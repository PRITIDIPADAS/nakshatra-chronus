II

Fair pairs: the SPHEREx rule

A linear variable filter gives each pixel its own color. Brightness can only be compared between exposures of the same array at nearly the same color. Each dot is one exposure. A line joins two visits that form a fair pair.

Calibration guardian

Release of every visit on every array, and the gain that the catalog stars measure. A release change or a gain step must not become a discovery.

III

XIII

How it works

From archive files to an evidence report.

A young man blinked two photographic plates and found Pluto. You cannot blink SPHEREx. Each pixel of each exposure sees a different colour of infrared light, so two pictures of one star can differ even when nothing in the sky changed.

CHRONOS compares only fair pairs, then asks five questions of every change: what changed, where, is it real, what is it, and what would prove us wrong. Three independent methods look for change. Where they disagree, CHRONOS investigates the disagreement.

For watchers of the changing sky like Radhagobinda Chandra of Jessore, who recorded variable stars by eye for decades, and for anyone who wants to check a claim instead of trusting it.

Candidate language only: nothing here is a confirmed discovery. Data from NASA IRSA SPHEREx, ESA Gaia and NASA WISE. Source code · 30 second demo
