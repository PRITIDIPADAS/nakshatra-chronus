# Pitch

## One line

You cannot blink SPHEREx, so CHRONOS compares only fair pairs, searches with three methods whose disagreement is measured, and tries to prove its own answer wrong.

## Three minute demo

1. **Hook (20 s).** Open the live observatory. Read the first line of the hero: a blink found Pluto, and SPHEREx cannot be blinked, because every pixel sees its own color.
2. **The SPHEREx rule (30 s).** Chapter II: the coverage plane. Lines join only fair pairs. Point at surveys 3 and 4, half a channel away.
3. **Visual proof (30 s).** Chapter III: step through the six layers. Raw difference, 27 spots. Star anchors, 10. Fair co add with honest noise, 1.
4. **The investigation (50 s).** Choose Barnard's Star. Chapter V on C04: hypotheses, the self questioning tests (the catalog motion of Barnard's Star agrees within 0.1 of its error), the counterfactual universe, the debate. Hover a reference in the report.
5. **Disagreement (20 s).** Back on the practice field, open the crosstalk candidate: all three methods agreed, the partner array saw nothing, a bright star sits 128 columns away. Verdict: artefact.
6. **Evidence (20 s).** Chapter VII: 98.8 percent when deciding, calibration error 0.007, 0 of 840 still stars moved.
7. **Planet X (10 s).** Chapter IX: the ladder; CHRONOS reaches the candidate rung and no further.

## Questions judges may ask

* **Is this real data?** The observatory runs on the practice sky by default and labels it SIMULATION. Live mode reads the IRSA archive with the same code; it is tested on files in the IRSA layout.
* **Why should I trust the probabilities?** They are calibrated on unseen practice fields and the engine says unknown when a case is unlike its training. On real data use the ranking and the unknown flag first.
* **What is new?** To my knowledge: fair pairs as a hard rule for SPHEREx change detection, disagreement between three detectors used as a calibrated signal, and tests chosen to break the leading explanation.
* **Does it find Planet X?** No. It can at most produce a candidate. NASA states that Planet X has not been discovered.
